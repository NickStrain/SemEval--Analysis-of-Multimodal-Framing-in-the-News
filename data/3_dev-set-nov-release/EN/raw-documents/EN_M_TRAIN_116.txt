Return of migrant workers signals economic revival in Kashmir Valley

Come spring, migrant labourers make a beeline for Kashmir, forming a vital workforce across construction, agriculture and the hospitality sector. Last year, however, the Pahalgam attack and subsequent military tensions between India and Pakistan prompted many workers to leave the Valley early or deterred others from arriving. This year, their return is reviving the Valley’s seasonal economy, with activity picking up at construction sites, orchards and small businesses that rely heavily on migrant labour.

Mansoor Ansari, a migrant labourer from Bihar, said he arrived on March 23 and more workers are expected in the coming days. “With the construction season starting in Kashmir, labourers, masons and carpenters have begun arriving,” Ansari said.

Last year, Ansari cut short his stay following the April 22 Pahalgam attack and Operation Sindoor. “We hope the situation remains peaceful and we can spend the entire working season here,” he added.

Sajad Ahmad, a contractor, said construction activity suffered last year due to a shortage of labourers. “This year, workers have started returning with the onset of the season, and we expect projects to pick up pace in the coming weeks,” he said.

The Ministry of Labour and Employment said that as of Nov. 26, 2025, a total of 3,611,005 unorganised workers, including migrant labourers, from Jammu and Kashmir had registered on the national e-Shram portal. According to the 2011 Census, migrant labourers account for about 80 per cent of Kashmir’s construction workforce, with an estimated 400,000 to 500,000 workers from states such as Bihar, West Bengal, Uttar Pradesh, Haryana and Punjab arriving in the Valley each year.

Despite multiple attacks, migrant labourers continue to return to the Valley for work. Data show that between 2019 and 2024, at least 36 migrant workers were killed in militant attacks in Jammu and Kashmir. The security situation has improved since, though concerns persist among workers.

Higher wages and relatively better working conditions are among the key reasons many migrant labourers choose to work in Kashmir. For many workers, seasonal migration sustains families back home through remittances, while local businesses and households depend on them for the timely completion of projects and services, underscoring the mutual reliance that drives the Valley’s economy.
